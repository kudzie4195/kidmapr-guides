Atlanta has one of the most robust charter school ecosystems in the South. For families who want a public school education outside their assigned school, charter lotteries are the primary path — but they move fast, have hard deadlines, and require planning that starts months earlier than most parents realize. This guide walks you through how lotteries work, which schools to know, and exactly how to apply.

## How charter lotteries work

Charter schools are publicly funded and tuition-free, but they are not required to enroll every student who applies. When more families apply than there are available seats, the school holds a **random lottery** to fill spots fairly. This process is required by Georgia law and overseen by the Georgia Department of Education.

The lottery system exists because charter schools cannot expand enrollment indefinitely — their budget is tied to the number of students they serve, and their facilities have fixed capacity. A strong charter school with a proven track record often has five to ten applications for every available kindergarten seat.

The typical Atlanta-area charter lottery follows this timeline: applications open in **January** (November for Atlanta Classical), close in **February**, and results are announced in **February or March** by email. Families who do not receive a spot are automatically placed on a ranked waitlist. Enrollment documents for offered seats are typically due within 48 to 72 hours of the offer.

## Major Atlanta-area charter schools

Each school below has its own application portal. Lottery windows and dates change annually — always verify on the school's website before October.

- **Drew Charter School** — East Lake neighborhood, PK–12. One of Atlanta's most recognized charter schools, with a strong arts and STEM integration. Lottery typically opens in January.
- **Atlanta Neighborhood Charter School (ANCS)** — Two campuses in Grant Park and Pittsburgh, K–8. Project-based learning, strong parent community. Lottery opens in January.
- **KIPP Metro Atlanta Schools** — Multiple campuses across the metro area, PK–12. College-preparatory mission with extended school day. One of the largest charter networks in Georgia. Lottery opens in January.
- **Atlanta Classical Academy** — Buckhead, K–12. Classical education model with Latin, logic, and rhetoric. **Lottery opens in November or December** — earlier than any other major Atlanta charter school. Do not miss this window.
- **Ivy Prep Academy** — Multiple metro-area locations, K–12. Georgia's largest network of all-girls public charter schools. Lottery opens in January or February.
- **Kindezi Schools** — Old Fourth Ward and Westside campuses, K–8. Community-centered model with strong family engagement. Lottery opens in January.
- **Odyssey School** — Fulton County, K–5. Themed around STEAM and project-based inquiry. Smaller campus with an intimate school culture. Lottery opens in January.
- **Global Village Project** — Decatur, serves refugee girls ages 11–17. Not a traditional K–5 program, but worth knowing for families with older children who are newly arrived. Rolling admissions.

## How to apply

**Research schools and note lottery dates.** Start in October of the year before your child would enter kindergarten. Visit each school's website and find the admissions or enrollment page. Write down the application open date, close date, and the date results are announced. Put Atlanta Classical on your list even if it is not your top choice — their early November window is easy to miss, and they are worth considering.

**Submit applications.** Georgia has no centralized charter school application system. You must apply to each school separately through their individual portal or paper form. Plan to apply to three to five schools. Most applications take 10 to 20 minutes and ask for basic household information, your child's date of birth, and your home address to confirm residency. Some schools charge a small application or waitlist deposit ($50–$200) to hold your place if you are offered a spot. This fee is not tuition — it is returned or credited toward enrollment.

**Wait for results.** Results are delivered by email. Most schools announce in February or early March. Check your spam folder — lottery result emails from small schools sometimes land there. You will receive one of two outcomes: an enrollment offer or a waitlist placement with your ranked position.

**Accept or join the waitlist.** If you receive an offer, you typically have 48 to 72 hours to accept and pay any required deposit. Do not let this window pass — missed offers go to the next family on the waitlist immediately. If you are waitlisted, do not assume the list will not move. Notify the school that you remain interested and ask for your waitlist position in writing.

**Complete enrollment.** Once you accept an offer, gather your enrollment documents: your child's original birth certificate, proof of Georgia residency (a utility bill, lease, or mortgage statement), your child's Georgia Form 3231 immunization record, and any required physical or health history forms. Many schools require these documents within one to two weeks of your acceptance.

## Waitlist strategy

Most Atlanta charter school waitlists move significantly between March and August. Here is why: families who receive multiple offers eventually choose one school and decline the others. A family at position 15 on the waitlist in March may realistically receive an offer by May or June as families who were offered spots choose elsewhere.

The families who land spots off waitlists are usually the ones who stay engaged. Contact the school's admissions office every three to four weeks. Send a brief, polite email asking about waitlist movement and reaffirming your interest. Keep your tone positive — admissions staff remember engaged families, and some schools (within legal limits) do consider expressed interest when making waitlist offers.

Do not give up on a waitlist before August. Many charter schools are still filling seats in late July as families who accepted spots decide to move, switch to private school, or relocate. If you receive a waitlist offer in July, it is valid — accept it if the school is still your preference.

## Do not skip public school registration

This is the most important practical note in this entire guide: **register for your assigned public school while you wait on charter lottery results.** Public school registration windows open between January and March — the same window as most charter lotteries. Do not delay your public school registration while waiting to see if you get a charter spot.

If you get your first-choice charter offer, you simply decline your public school registration. But if all your charter applications go to waitlist, you want your assigned school registration already active. Some families have found themselves scrambling for a school placement in August because they skipped public school registration while waiting on charters. Keep both paths open until you have a confirmed enrollment.
