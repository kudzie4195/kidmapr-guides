Private school tuition in Georgia can run $8,000 to $25,000 per year — a price that puts it out of reach for most families. But Georgia's scholarship tax credit program, commonly called GOAL, has quietly made private K-12 education accessible to tens of thousands of families who never expected to afford it. This guide explains how the program works, which organization is right for your family, and exactly how to apply.

## What is the GOAL Scholarship?

GOAL stands for Georgia's Own private school Assistance Lottery — but it is not a lottery in the traditional sense. It is a **scholarship tax credit program** authorized by the Georgia General Assembly. Here is how the money flows: Georgia businesses and individuals donate to approved organizations called **Student Scholarship Organizations (SSOs)**. In return, donors receive a Georgia income tax credit equal to the amount of their donation. The SSOs use those donations to fund scholarships for Georgia children attending accredited private schools.

The program has a statewide annual cap — currently set at **$120 million per year** — which means total SSO funding is limited. Once the cap is reached, no new tax credit donations are authorized for that calendar year. This is why applying early in January matters so much: the best-funded SSOs can exhaust their scholarship budgets before summer.

The program is available for students in kindergarten through 12th grade. **Pre-K is not eligible.** Homeschool families are also not eligible — the scholarship must go to a student enrolled at an accredited private school.

## How much can you receive?

Award amounts vary by SSO and, for income-based programs, by household size and income. Here is a general breakdown for the 2025-26 award cycle:

- **Georgia GOAL** — approximately $4,800–$5,500 per year for K-12 students. No income requirement. Awards are similar for all families regardless of income.
- **GRACE Scholars** — approximately $4,500–$9,500 per year. Income-based: families with lower household incomes receive higher award amounts. Priority given to households earning below 200% of the federal poverty level.
- **Apogee Scholarship** — approximately $5,000–$8,500 per year. Income-based, statewide coverage, with strong participation from schools outside the metro Atlanta area.

These figures are estimates. Each SSO publishes its current award schedule on its website, and amounts are adjusted annually.

## Which SSO is right for you?

Choosing the right SSO depends on your income and how quickly you can apply.

**Georgia GOAL** is the right choice if your household income is moderate to high, or if you simply want the fastest path to an award. Georgia GOAL does not require any income documentation. Applications are accepted online starting in January, and awards are granted on a first-come, first-served basis until funds run out. If speed is your priority, Georgia GOAL wins.

**GRACE Scholars** is the right choice if your household income is lower and you want the largest possible award. GRACE prioritizes families with demonstrated financial need, and its award ceiling ($9,500) is significantly higher than Georgia GOAL's. The tradeoff: you will need to provide income documentation (tax returns or W-2s), and the application process takes longer.

**Apogee Scholarship** serves families across the state — not just metro Atlanta — and is income-based with a competitive award ceiling. If you live outside Atlanta and your chosen private school participates in Apogee's network, it is worth applying here in addition to or instead of the Atlanta-centric SSOs.

You may apply to more than one SSO. If you receive multiple offers, you must choose one — scholarships cannot be stacked across SSOs.

## Step-by-step application

**Step 1: Choose your SSO.** Review the three options above and select the one that best fits your income level and timeline. If you are uncertain, start with Georgia GOAL for speed while also applying to GRACE Scholars for a potentially higher award.

**Step 2: Confirm your school is eligible.** Not every accredited private school participates in every SSO's network. Before you apply, use the school search tool on your chosen SSO's website to verify that your target school is enrolled in their program. If the school is not listed, contact them directly — some schools participate but are not prominently listed.

**Step 3: Create an account and apply.** Visit your SSO's website and complete the online application. Georgia GOAL's application is entirely online and requires no income documentation — only basic household information and your child's school choice. GRACE Scholars and Apogee will ask you to upload a recent tax return or W-2. Have those documents scanned and ready before you start.

**Step 4: Receive your award letter.** Once your application is approved, your SSO will send an award letter by email. Do not send this letter to the school directly — contact the school's business office or financial aid office, let them know you have a GOAL Scholarship award letter, and ask for their process. Funds are disbursed directly from the SSO to the school, not to you.

**Step 5: Renew each January.** GOAL Scholarships are awarded one academic year at a time. You must re-apply each January for the following school year. Set a calendar reminder for January 2 — Georgia GOAL in particular can exhaust its funding within days of the application window opening.

## Important things to know

**GOAL Scholarships can be combined with school-based financial aid.** If your private school offers its own merit scholarships or need-based grants, a GOAL Scholarship does not disqualify you from those awards. Many families layer GOAL plus school aid to make tuition genuinely affordable.

**The award goes to the school, not to your bank account.** This is not a cash payment. The SSO sends funds directly to the school on your behalf. You will not receive a check.

**Pre-K is not eligible.** The scholarship covers kindergarten through 12th grade only. If your child is currently in Pre-K and you are hoping to use GOAL next year for kindergarten, you are on the right track — apply in January of the year your child will enter kindergarten.

**You must renew every year.** Missing the January renewal window can mean losing your scholarship for that school year. Many families have had to pay full tuition for a year because they forgot to renew on time.
