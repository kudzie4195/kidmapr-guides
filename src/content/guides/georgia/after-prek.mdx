The end of Georgia's Pre-K year is a milestone worth celebrating — and a planning deadline you cannot afford to miss. By the time your child graduates from their Pre-K classroom, you should already have a kindergarten plan in place. This guide walks you through every option Georgia families have, the timeline that keeps you on track, and the practical steps to secure your child's spot.

## Three paths forward

Georgia families choosing a kindergarten placement have three main options, and most will want to explore all three at the same time.

**Public school** is the guaranteed baseline. Every Georgia child is assigned to a public elementary school based on home address. Registration is free, enrollment is a right, and your assigned school must accept your child. This is the path of least resistance — and for many families, it is the best one.

**Charter school lottery** is the route for families who want a public school alternative without paying tuition. Charter schools are publicly funded but independently operated, and they admit students by random lottery when applications exceed available seats. Atlanta is home to a strong network of charter schools with distinctive missions and strong academic records. The catch: spots are competitive and lotteries are time-limited.

**GOAL Scholarship** opens the door to private K-12 education for families who previously assumed it was out of reach financially. Georgia's scholarship tax credit program funds awards of $3,000–$10,000 per year through organizations called Student Scholarship Organizations (SSOs). You apply to the SSO, not to the state, and awards go directly to the school of your choice.

## Your planning timeline

Start earlier than you think you need to. Here is a month-by-month guide from your child's final Pre-K spring through their first day of kindergarten.

- **January (of Pre-K year):** Georgia GOAL Scholarship applications open — apply immediately if you are considering private school. Atlanta charter school lottery windows begin opening. Start your list of schools to research.
- **January–February:** Submit charter school lottery applications. Most lotteries are open for only a few weeks.
- **January–March:** Public school kindergarten registration opens in most Georgia counties. Register at your assigned school even if you are pursuing charter or private options.
- **February–March:** Charter lottery results are announced. You will receive either an enrollment offer or automatic waitlist placement.
- **March–April:** Accept charter or private school offers. Complete enrollment paperwork, including immunization records (Form 3231) and proof of residency.
- **May:** Confirm your plan. If waitlisted at a charter school, continue to check in every few weeks — waitlists move.
- **June–July:** Attend kindergarten orientation if offered. Arrange before- and after-school care for your chosen school.
- **August:** First day of kindergarten.

## Public school registration

Georgia children are entitled to enroll in their assigned public school at no cost. Your assigned school is determined by your home address. Registration windows vary by county but typically open between January and March. You will need your child's birth certificate, proof of Georgia residency (a utility bill or lease), and their Georgia Form 3231 immunization record. Find your county's registration page by searching your county name plus "kindergarten registration" — for example, "Fulton County kindergarten registration 2026" or "Gwinnett County kindergarten enrollment." If you are unsure which school your address is assigned to, your county school system's website will have a school finder tool.

## Charter school lottery

Atlanta's charter school ecosystem is one of the strongest in the Southeast, and several schools have waitlists that stretch years into the future. Major schools to consider include **Drew Charter School** (East Lake, PK-12), **Atlanta Neighborhood Charter School** (Grant Park and Pittsburgh campuses, K-8), **KIPP Metro Atlanta** (multiple campuses, PK-12), **Atlanta Classical Academy** (Buckhead, K-12), **Ivy Prep Academy** (girls-focused, multiple locations, K-12), and **Kindezi Schools** (Old Fourth Ward and Westside, K-8). Most lottery windows open in January and close by mid-February, with results announced in February or March. Atlanta Classical is an exception — their lottery typically opens in November or December. There is no central application; you must apply to each school separately, and there is no penalty for applying to multiple schools simultaneously.

## GOAL Scholarship

If private school tuition has felt out of reach, the Georgia GOAL Scholarship may change the math. The program is funded through Georgia's scholarship tax credit — businesses and individuals redirect tax dollars to Student Scholarship Organizations, which then award scholarships to qualifying families. Awards range from approximately **$3,000 to $10,000 per year** depending on the SSO and your household income. Two of the largest SSOs in Georgia are **Georgia GOAL** (georgiagoal.org), which awards scholarships on a first-come, first-served basis with no income requirement, and **GRACE Scholars** (gracescholars.org), which targets lower-income families with higher award amounts. Kindergarten is eligible; Pre-K is not. Apply as early as possible in January — Georgia GOAL funding can run out before spring.

## Is your child ready for kindergarten?

Georgia uses the **Georgia Early Learning and Development Standards (GELDS)** to describe what children should know and be able to do as they enter kindergarten. These standards span language and literacy, math, science, social studies, and social-emotional development. Most children entering kindergarten are not expected to read fluently — they are expected to recognize letters, count to 20, follow simple instructions, and manage basic social interactions like taking turns and asking for help. If you have concerns about your child's readiness, speak with their Pre-K teacher before the school year ends. Many county school systems also offer kindergarten readiness screenings in the spring.

## Need before or after-school care?

Most elementary schools in Georgia do not offer extended care built into the school day. If you work outside the home, you will need to arrange before- or after-school care separately. Some schools operate their own programs; others partner with providers. Browse licensed childcare centers near your child's school at [CloverMap](https://clovermap.com) to find before- and after-school programs, filter by age range and availability, and compare options before the school year begins. Start this search early — spots in school-age care programs fill up just as quickly as infant and toddler slots.
