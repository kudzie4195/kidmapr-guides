Childcare costs in Georgia are among the largest line items in a working family's budget — often exceeding rent or a car payment. The **Childcare and Parent Services (CAPS)** program is Georgia's childcare subsidy, funded jointly by the federal Child Care and Development Fund and the state. It pays a portion of childcare costs directly to your chosen provider so you can work, attend school, or complete job training. This guide explains who qualifies, what documents you need, and exactly how to apply.

## What is CAPS?

CAPS is administered by the Georgia Department of Early Care and Learning (DECAL). The program pays a childcare subsidy on behalf of qualifying families to licensed, CAPS-approved childcare providers. The subsidy covers a portion of the provider's rate — you pay the remainder as a co-payment, which is based on your household income and size. Families with lower incomes pay smaller co-payments; the program covers more of the cost.

CAPS is not an entitlement program. Funding is capped, and in periods of high demand some counties maintain a waitlist. Applying early and completely — with all required documents — gives you the best chance of timely approval.

## Who qualifies?

To be eligible for CAPS, your household must meet all of the following:

- **Activity requirement:** You must be working, attending school or a job training program, or actively participating in a TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families) work activity. Two-parent households must have both parents meet this requirement, unless one parent has a documented disability.
- **Income requirement:** Your gross household income must fall within current CAPS income limits, which are updated annually. Limits are based on household size and are expressed as a percentage of the state median income. Check [caps.decal.ga.gov](https://caps.decal.ga.gov) for the current income table.
- **Georgia residency:** Your child must live with you in Georgia.
- **Child age:** CAPS covers children from birth through age 12, or through age 17 for children with special needs.

Single-parent households, foster families, and families receiving TANF may qualify under adjusted thresholds. If you are unsure whether you qualify, apply — the eligibility determination is made by DECAL, not by you.

## The application process

**Step 1: Check eligibility.** Before you apply, verify that your household income is within the current CAPS income limits and that your work or school activity qualifies. If you were recently laid off, CAPS has a limited grace period — check with DECAL about your specific situation.

**Step 2: Choose a CAPS-approved provider.** CAPS only pays for childcare at **licensed, CAPS-approved centers and family childcare homes**. Not all licensed daycares accept CAPS — the reimbursement rates are set by DECAL and some providers choose not to participate. Confirm with your chosen provider before you apply. Browse [CloverMap](https://clovermap.com) to find licensed childcare centers near you and filter for CAPS-accepting locations, or use the provider search tool at [caps.decal.ga.gov](https://caps.decal.ga.gov).

**Step 3: Gather your documents.** Collect the following before you start your application: a valid photo ID for the applying parent or guardian, proof of Georgia residency (utility bill, lease, or bank statement with your address), proof of income for all working adults in the household (most recent pay stubs and most recent tax return), your child's birth certificate, and your child's Georgia Form 3231 immunization record. If you are enrolled in school or a job training program, gather proof of enrollment as well.

**Step 4: Submit your application online.** Apply through the CAPS online portal at [caps.decal.ga.gov](https://caps.decal.ga.gov). You will create an account, complete the application form, and upload your documents. Save your application confirmation number — you will need it to check your status. The portal is available 24 hours a day; you do not need to apply during business hours.

**Step 5: Attend an eligibility interview if required.** Some Georgia counties require a brief eligibility interview before your application is approved. If your county requires one, DECAL will contact you by phone or email to schedule it. Be responsive — delays in responding to interview requests can delay your approval. Bring or have available all original documents.

**Step 6: Receive your authorization and enroll.** When your application is approved, you will receive a CAPS authorization. Share this authorization number with your childcare provider immediately. **Providers cannot bill CAPS until they have your authorization number** — your subsidy does not begin until enrollment is confirmed. Review your co-payment amount with your provider so you know exactly what you owe each week or month.

## What happens after approval

CAPS authorizations are not permanent. You must **recertify your eligibility** periodically — typically every 6 to 12 months — to continue receiving the subsidy. DECAL will notify you when recertification is due. Submit your updated income documents and activity verification on time to avoid a gap in coverage.

If your income, household size, employment status, or childcare provider changes, report the change to DECAL promptly. Failing to report changes can result in overpayment, which you may be required to repay.

If you are placed on a CAPS waitlist, ask for your position number and check in regularly. Waitlists move as funding is added and families cycle off.

## Finding a CAPS-approved childcare provider

The fastest way to find a licensed, CAPS-accepting center near you is to browse [CloverMap](https://clovermap.com). Filter your search by CAPS acceptance, age range, and location to see available centers with current openings. You can also use the CAPS provider directory at [caps.decal.ga.gov](https://caps.decal.ga.gov) to search by county and zip code. Contact providers directly to confirm they have space before you finalize your application — CAPS approval is tied to a specific provider, and you will need to update your file if you switch centers.
